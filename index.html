About

I study how infectious disease surveillance and inference can be improved when infection processes are only partially observed. My work focuses on wildlife and multi-host systems, using spatiotemporal epidemiology, Bayesian methods, and mechanistic models to understand transmission processes and support better surveillance decisions.

Research

Quantitative infectious disease epidemiology across wildlife, livestock, and human systems, with a focus on transmission and surveillance at their interfaces.

Current research programme
Surveillance Data Science

From imperfect observations to better surveillance decisions: infer latent infection risk, evaluate the added value of new host or data sources, and design what to observe next under limited surveillance resources.
